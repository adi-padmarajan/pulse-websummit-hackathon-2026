## The Pitch

It is day two of Web Summit.

You just walked out of a session that completely changed how you think. Your next talk starts in 60 minutes. Around you are 2000+ founders, investors, engineers, operators, students, and creators.

So you walk into the lobby, look around, and do what almost everyone else is doing.

You scroll your phone.

> **That coffee break was one of the most expensive hours of your trip. And you spent it alone.**

The right people are already here. They may have just left the same session. They may be thinking about the same problem. They may be nearby right now, hoping to meet someone exactly like you.

But you still do not know who to talk to.

Most event networking tools are built like static directories. They ask you to search profiles, send cold messages, and maybe schedule something later.

But live events are not static. They are made of moments.

The person you should meet might only be relevant for the next 30 minutes, before you both disappear into different sessions, lunch plans, meetings, or afterparties.

> **The real problem is not discovery. The real problem is timing.**

That is why we built Pulse.

<br>

> ### Pulse does not ask, “Who should I connect with someday?”
> ### Pulse asks, “Who should I talk to right now?”

## What Pulse Does

Pulse is a real-time networking layer for Web Summit.

It helps attendees turn the empty time between sessions into meaningful conversations by showing who nearby is relevant to their current context, interest, and available time.

Instead of browsing an attendee list, users create a short-lived **Quest**.

A Quest says:

- What you want to talk about
- Where your head is right now
- How much time you have
- Whether you are open to a one-on-one conversation or a small gathering

Pulse then shows nearby people whose context overlaps with yours.

You can wave, meet, save the conversation, follow up on LinkedIn, and return to the loop with a new Quest.

## The Problem

Web Summit creates enormous opportunity by bringing thousands of people into the same place.

But for attendees, that opportunity is hard to use.

You might know what session you just attended. You might know what you want to talk about. You might know that you have 45 minutes before your next event.

But you do not know:

- Who nearby is thinking about the same thing
- Who is also available right now
- Who would actually be open to talking
- Which conversations are worth saving afterward

So the most valuable moments at live events often disappear.

Not because people do not want to connect.

Because the room has no interface.

---

## The Solution

Pulse gives the room an interface.

Attendees broadcast short-lived intent through Quests. Pulse then visualizes nearby matches through a live Radar, explains why each match is relevant, and helps people form small, useful conversations in the moment.

After the conversation, Pulse asks whether the meeting actually happened and whether it is worth saving. If it was valuable, Pulse records it as a saved conversation, shows a LinkedIn follow-up path, and lets the attendee review their day through **See All My Quests**.

Pulse is not trying to replace Web Summit Mobile App.

It makes the Web Summit room work better.

## How Pulse Works

### 1. Create a Quest

A Quest is a temporary signal of what you want from the room right now.

You can create a Quest after a session, before lunch, during a break, or any time you want to meet someone relevant.

Examples:

> “Just left the AI product session. Looking for someone building pricing experiments around AI tools.”

> “Have 45 minutes before my next talk. Want to meet another founder working on fan engagement or live events.”

> “Heading to lunch with no plan. Open to joining a small group of people building consumer apps.”

Quests expire automatically, so Pulse stays focused on the present moment.

---

### 2. Open the Radar

The Radar is a live spatial view of nearby people whose Quests overlap with yours.

Instead of scrolling through profiles, you see the room as a living network of opportunities.

Matches appear based on:

- Shared session context
- Topic overlap
- Proximity
- Available time window
- Mutual openness to talk
- Conversation intent

The Radar is designed to feel active and alive. People appear and disappear as Quests expire, making the experience feel more like watching the room think than browsing a directory.

---

### 3. Understand the Match

When you tap a match, Pulse explains why the person may be worth your next conversation.

Instead of only showing a profile, Pulse gives you:

- A short reason for the match
- The shared context
- Their current Quest
- A suggested opener

Example:

> “You both just left sessions about AI product design and are interested in pricing experiments. They are looking for someone who has tested real user willingness to pay.”

Suggested opener:

> “I saw you’re thinking about AI pricing experiments. Are you testing pricing with real users yet, or still figuring out the model?”

The goal is to remove the awkward first sentence.

---

### 4. Wave

If someone seems relevant, you send a Wave.

A Wave is a lightweight request to connect.

If they wave back, the connection forms.

Identity stays hidden until both sides agree to meet. This keeps Pulse intentional, private, and safe.

---

### 5. Meet or Grow a Gathering

Two people connecting is only the start.

If a conversation looks useful, Pulse can grow it into a small Gathering.

Nearby attendees with overlapping context can request to join, turning one conversation into a small table of people who actually share something.

This helps create the kind of spontaneous group conversations that make live events valuable, without relying on random mixers or awkward name-tag networking.

---

### 6. Conversation Check-In

After the meeting or Gathering, Pulse shows a **Conversation Check-In** screen.

Pulse asks whether the conversation happened and whether it should be saved.

The attendee can choose:

- **Save this conversation**
- **It did not happen**

If the conversation happened and was useful, Pulse saves it into the attendee’s Quest history.

If the conversation did not happen, Pulse does not add it to the saved conversation list.

This keeps the recap clean. Pulse does not record every attempted match. It only saves conversations that actually happened and were worth remembering.

---

### 7. LinkedIn Follow-Up

After saving a conversation, Pulse shows the other person’s LinkedIn profile and gives the attendee the option to connect.

This turns the live-event moment into a lasting professional relationship.

Pulse helps attendees find and remember the right conversation.

LinkedIn helps them continue it after Web Summit.

---

### 8. See All My Quests

After saving or skipping a conversation, the attendee can tap **See All My Quests**.

This screen shows the conversations they saved during the day, including who they met, what the conversation was about, and any follow-up actions such as LinkedIn.

From there, the attendee can tap **New Quest** to return to the beginning of the loop and start looking for the next conversation.

Pulse becomes a repeatable event networking loop:

> **Quest → Conversation → Recap → New Quest**

## Why Pulse Is Different

### It matches moments, not profiles

Most networking apps match who you are.

Pulse matches what you need right now.

The signal is temporary, intent-driven, and tied to a real time window.

---

### It is built for timing

At live events, the best conversation is often only possible for the next 30 minutes.

Pulse focuses on who is relevant, nearby, available, and open to talking now.

---

### It builds conversations, not just contacts

Pulse is not just about collecting names.

It helps attendees find useful conversations, form small Gatherings, save the ones that mattered, and follow up afterward.

---

### It is designed against the creepy version

A real-time networking app could easily become uncomfortable.

Pulse is designed to avoid that.

- Quests expire automatically
- Identity stays hidden until both sides agree
- Location is shown as proximity, not exact position
- People only become visible through mutual networking intent
- Conversations are saved only after confirmation

The product is built around consent, timing, and relevance.

## Three Ways to Drop a Quest

| Mode | When to Use It | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Just attended a session** | For the moment right after a talk, panel, or masterclass | “Just left the AI design session and want to compare notes with someone building AI products.” |
| **Want to talk about a topic** | For when you have a specific idea on your mind | “Looking for someone testing pricing models for AI tools. Happy to trade real experiments.” |
| **Just in a moment** | For lunch, coffee breaks, or open time | “Have 40 minutes before my next session. Open to joining a small founder lunch group.” |

## Tools and Services Used

- **Frontend:** React, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, shadcn/ui, Framer Motion
- **Backend:** Supabase
- **Database:** PostgreSQL with realtime subscriptions and Row Level Security
- **AI Layer:** Claude for Quest parsing and match explanation generation
- **Deployment:** Lovable
- **Visualization:** SVG-based Radar with Framer Motion animations
- **Follow-Up Layer:** LinkedIn profile handoff flow
